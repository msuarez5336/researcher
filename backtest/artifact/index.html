How prices are estimated, and how far to trust them

  • Trading cost is assumed. Options have a gap between the buying and selling price. This page assumes the gap is 8% of the option's price and that you give up three quarters of it, both when buying and when selling before expiry.
  • Survivors only. The stocks are today's most actively traded S&P 500 members, so none went bankrupt or was delisted. That flatters calls and penalises puts.
  • No earnings premium. Real options cost more ahead of earnings reports. The estimate does not know when they fall.
  • After a sharp fall, real options get more expensive faster than this estimate does. That flatters the fall-and-rebound setups in particular.
  • Checked against only five very large companies. The estimate may be further off for smaller or more volatile stocks.
  • Simplifications. Any strike is available, dividends are ignored, and the purchase is at the closing price on the day after the setup appears.
  • Significance. The t-statistic is computed on monthly averages and divided by the square root of the holding period in months, because overlapping trades are not independent. A value of 3 or more is the bar; below that, treat a result as chance.
