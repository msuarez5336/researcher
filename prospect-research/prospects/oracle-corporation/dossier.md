## Summary
- **Who:** a $67.4bn-revenue software and cloud company, US-headquartered (Austin, Texas), with about
  141,000 staff, in the middle of an AI-linked restructuring under new co-CEOs and a new CFO.
- **Strongest reason to pitch:** Oracle has cut about 21,000 net roles in a year (US: about 9,000),
  added about $700m to its restructuring plan after 31 August 2026, and its own annual report warns
  this may cause skill shortages and retention damage. That is a talent-strategy problem in Oracle's own words.
- **Biggest risk:** Oracle is sophisticated in-house. It sells HR software (Fusion Cloud HCM) and runs its
  own talent reviews on it. Its board already retains Compensia as the independent pay adviser.
- **Fit:** talent strategy **moderate to strong**; compensation strategy **moderate** for broad-based pay,
  **poor** for executive pay.
- **Next step:** lead with talent strategy for the AI-driven workforce reshaping. First find out who
  owns talent strategy, since no head of HR appears in the filings.

**Fit: moderate to strong.** The need is stated and current. The risks are Oracle's in-house strength
and an unidentified buyer.

**Value hypothesis [inference]:** Oracle is shrinking its legacy workforce and adding data-centre, AI
and power roles. It has said this risks skill shortages and retention damage. Talent strategy can
help it decide which skills to keep, retrain and hire, so the cuts don't take out capability it
needs for the $90–95bn build-out.

**Hooks:**
1. "Your 10-K says AI adoption may keep reducing headcount and that restructuring risks skill
   shortages and retention damage. How are you deciding which capabilities to protect?" (W3-13, W3-11)
2. "You added about $700m to the restructuring plan after August. What does the next wave look
   like for the US workforce, which fell from 58,000 to 49,000?" (L-1, L-3, W1-3)
3. "Five data-centre campuses for OpenAI need people who don't work at Oracle today. Where is that
   talent coming from?" (W2-9; hiring per W2-10 ~)
