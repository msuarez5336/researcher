# Compensation strategy consulting

- What it does, in one or two sentences: Designs pay philosophy, salary structures, incentive and equity plans, and executive pay; benchmarks pay against peers; supports pay-equity and pay-transparency compliance.
- Problem it solves for the client: Pay that is uncompetitive, inequitable, too costly, out of line with shareholder expectations, or not compliant with pay-transparency laws.
- Signs that a company needs it: low or falling say-on-pay support; proxy-advisor criticism; large equity grants or dilution concerns; restructuring or layoffs; new pay-transparency laws in states where it hires; pay-equity litigation; a new CEO or CHRO.
- Who usually buys it (functions or titles): Chief People Officer / CHRO, Head of Total Rewards or Compensation; for executive pay, the board's compensation committee.
- Who else is involved in the decision: CFO, General Counsel, procurement.
- What it replaces (a competitor, an in-house team, doing nothing): the incumbent compensation consultant (executive-pay advisers are named in the proxy statement), the in-house total rewards team.
