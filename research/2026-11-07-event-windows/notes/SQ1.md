# SQ1 notes (as of 2026-11-07)
## Answer
Reversals after large no-news moves are documented (Chan 2003, Savor 2012) and stronger in high-VIX regimes (Nagel 2012), but they are concentrated in small, low-turnover stocks; in large/liquid stocks 1-month moves tend to continue (Medhat-Schmeling 2022) and net-of-cost reversal profit is thin. No primary study of buying options or debit spreads after shocks was found; vendor/blog material suggests IV spikes decay quickly but can re-spike.
## Findings
- Reversal vs turnover/size: low-turnover reverses, high-turnover continues; large-cap gross 0.84%/mo but costs 1.94%/mo subsume (microcap-heavy) [E] SQ1-1,2,3
- Costs: gross alpha >6%/yr, net < -2% at 25bp/trip with >1000% turnover [E] SQ1-4
- Regime: reversal profit predictable by VIX [E] SQ1-5
- News vs no-news: no-info events reverse, info events drift [E] SQ1-6 (Chan SQ1-7 unverified detail)
- IV decay: VIX 30->20 in 8 days one episode; re-spikes within days common [E/C] SQ1-8,9. A ~20-trading-day VIX shock half-life figure appeared only in a search summary (not cached, not recorded).
- Vendor: SPY IV spikes >=5pts then +5.12% 20d SPY return, overlapping, vendor [E, B] SQ1-10
- Options after jumps: index IV adjusts gradually after jumps; no abnormal returns after costs (snippet only) SQ1-11
## Conflicts
Reversal (Chan, Savor, Nagel; small/illiquid, no news, high VIX) vs momentum in liquid/high-turnover stocks (Medhat-Schmeling). Market-wide: IVolatility bounce +5.12%/20d vs re-spike risk.
