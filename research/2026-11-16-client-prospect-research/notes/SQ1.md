# SQ1 notes (as of 2026-11-16)

## Answer
Qualification frameworks (MEDDIC/MEDDPICC, BANT) and practitioner pre-call checklists agree on a core: who the company is, what pain or priority it has, who decides and how, what it already uses, and why now. Which of these matter, and how deep to go, depends on the service. Only the "who the company is" block is service-independent. I found no measured evidence that research depth raises win or reply rates, and no framework source on how to establish the US footprint of a global company.

## Findings
- [O] MEDDPICC has eight criteria: Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Identify Pain, Champion, Competition (SQ1-1). Economic Buyer is the budget holder (SQ1-2). Paper Process is the legal, security and procurement path (SQ1-3). Pain needs a stated cost of inaction (SQ1-4). Competition includes the status quo and build-in-house (SQ1-5).
- [O] BANT is Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline (SQ1-6). It is criticised as dated (SQ1-7). One vendor reframes Authority as "who is involved in the decision" (SQ1-8).
- [O] Practitioner checklists: new leaders as a trigger (SQ1-9); existing vendor decides displacement vs greenfield (SQ1-10); earnings calls as a source of priorities (SQ1-11); a value hypothesis ties a priority to the service (SQ1-12).
- [O] Checklists differ by offering: a commercial-services checklist says SaaS-style checklists do not fit (SQ1-13).
- [E, weak] Only a perception survey exists: 91.6% of respondents said pre-meeting research significantly affected call success (SQ1-14). It is not a win rate, comes from a vendor, and is unverified (not in the cached page).

Champion and Metrics are deal-stage items that need live conversations, so desk research cannot fill them (my inference, not a source claim).

## Inputs needed up front
- Company name, plus website or domain to disambiguate.
- Country of HQ, and whether a US presence is expected.
- The service offered: description, who buys it, typical price band, who it replaces.
- Goal of the research (first meeting, account plan, or go/no-go).
- Known contacts, existing relationship history, and the ICP criteria (the last two are common in checklists; I did not capture a quote).

## Conflicts
- BANT (seller-centric, single buyer) vs MEDDPICC (buying committee, process). Sources are vendors who sell alternatives, so the "BANT is dead" claim is opinion. BANT fits quick qualification of small or simple deals. MEDDPICC fits complex enterprise deals (SQ1-7, SQ1-8, SQ1-1).
- Gartner buying-group size figures conflict across secondary blogs (6-10, 11, 5-16). No primary figure was verified, so none is recorded.
- Origin of BANT is given as the 1950s or the 1960s depending on the source. Not material here.
