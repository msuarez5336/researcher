## 5. Questions the research must answer (fixed)
Service-independent (answered once per client):
1. Who is the company: legal name, parent and ownership, industry, size (revenue, headcount), leadership.
2. US footprint (for non-US headquarters): US legal entity, US locations and headcount, US leadership, and whether buying decisions for these services are made in the US.
3. Dated signals from the last 6–12 months: leadership changes, funding, M&A, earnings priorities, launches, layoffs, expansion.

Per service in section 2:
4. Pain or initiative the service addresses, in the client's own words where possible, and the cost of not acting.
5. What they use today: named vendor, in-house team or nothing.
6. Budget signals and the likely budget holder.
7. Who is involved in the decision (functions, titles) and how the client buys (procurement, legal, security steps).
8. Timing: a deadline or event that makes this urgent.
9. Fit: strong, moderate or poor against section 3, with the evidence for each criterion.
10. Value hypothesis and 2–3 specific, sourced conversation hooks.

Always: every fact cited and dated; inference labelled as inference; "not found" stated, never guessed.
Not answerable from desk research (ask in the meeting): the client's success metrics and who will champion the purchase.

## 6. Source rules (fixed)
- Use: the client's own site (about, careers, press, investor pages), SEC EDGAR through its data APIs, USAspending, public job-board APIs, news.
- Needs a free key: SAM.gov opportunities.
- Do not automate: LinkedIn, the Delaware entity search.
- People: business role and public statements only.
