## Top findings
1. **No official source found requires U.S. citizenship, but none rules it out either.** The DSHS
   certification page and the certification rule (25 TAC 157.33) list age, education, course,
   exams and fingerprints, and say nothing about citizenship, lawful presence or a Social Security
   number. [E] ([DSHS](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/dshs-ems-trauma-systems/ems-personnel-certification-licensure/initial-certification-licensure),
   [25 TAC 157.33](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/25-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-157-33)) ✓
2. **The likely obstacles for non-citizens are the SSN and ID steps, not a stated rule.**
   The NREMT profile displays the last four digits of an SSN, though no NREMT page says one is
   required. Since January 2026 another Texas licensing agency (TDLR) has required proof of
   lawful presence under federal law 8 U.S.C. 1621. No equivalent DSHS notice was found. [E] ([NREMT](https://nremt.org/Document/Learn-More),
   [TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/news/2026/01/26/lawful-presence-documentation-requirement/)) ✓
3. **Everyone needs:** age 18+, a high school diploma or GED (foreign diplomas evaluated), a
   DSHS-approved course, the NREMT exam, a state jurisprudence exam, and an FBI fingerprint check,
   all within two years of finishing the course. [E] ([25 TAC 157.33](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/25-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-157-33)) ✓
4. **Cheapest paid courses found:** Alvin Community College at about $871 for in-district
   residents, and Dallas EMT at $995 for anyone. Add about $168 in certification fees
   (DSHS $64, plus NREMT $104 per exam attempt) and the fingerprinting fee. [E] ([Alvin CC](https://alvincollege.edu/ems),
   [Dallas EMT](https://dallasemt.com/emt-training-costs/)) ✓
5. **Free route:** a state grant (TWC) pays tuition and the NREMT fee for EMT-Basic trainees,
   but the money goes to an EMS employer, so you need an ambulance service to sponsor you.
   Applications run in rolling windows: open two months, then closed one. The last window
   listed on the page closed on October 31, 2026. [E] ([TWC](https://www.twc.texas.gov/emergency-medical-response-service-staffing-program)) ✓

## 1. Who qualifies

### What the rules require
| Requirement | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Age | "You must be at least 18 years old" to be certified. Austin CC also requires 18 on the first day of class; TEEX accepts 17 to enrol; FASTems says 17 with parental consent. | [DSHS](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/dshs-ems-trauma-systems/ems-personnel-certification-licensure/initial-certification-licensure) ✓, [ACC](https://sites.austincc.edu/health/emsp/faq/) ✓, [FASTems](https://www.fastemsts.com/accelerated-emt-course/) ✓ |

| Course | A DSHS-approved course; its number starts with 6 and is on the completion document. | DSHS ✓ |
| Exams | The NREMT exam (it can be taken before applying to the state) and a state jurisprudence exam. The DSHS page omits the jurisprudence exam; the rule requires it. | DSHS ✓, 157.33 ✓ |
| Criminal history | Fingerprints for a Texas/FBI check through IdentoGO. Denial is **mandatory** for a listed set of serious offenses, including murder, aggravated kidnapping, sexual assault, aggravated robbery and certain drug offenses, and for sex-offender-registrable offenses committed on or after September 1, 2009. Other offenses related to EMS duties are judged case by case. DSHS offers a prescreening before you enrol, for $50 plus the fingerprint fee. | DSHS ✓, [25 TAC 157.37](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/25-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-157-37) ✓ |
| Deadline | All requirements within two years of course completion. | 157.33 ✓ |
| Fees | DSHS application $64 (the DSHS page; the rule copy, amended only through 2019, says $60). NREMT is a private national registry, not a government body; its fee is $104 per exam attempt. The fingerprint fee was not found on an official page (RC Health's 2018 sheet listed $48). | DSHS ✓, [NREMT](https://nremt.org/Handbooks/EMT/Certification-Process) ✓ |

### Citizenship, immigration status and Social Security number
- **DSHS and the rule text:** no mention of citizenship, lawful presence or an SSN in the
  pages read. [E] ✓ That is silence, not permission.
- **NREMT:** its account page shows "the last 4 digits of the social security number associated
  with that account". No NREMT page was found that says an SSN is mandatory or describes an
  alternative. [E] ✓ Massachusetts says NREMT issues a "pseudo SSN" to people without one. ~
  (search snippet; mass.gov refused access)
- **Exam day:** Pearson VUE requires a government-issued photo ID that exactly matches the
  booking name. A foreign passport is not ruled out by the page. [E] ✓
- **Other Texas agencies:** since January 26, 2026, TDLR requires certain applicants to prove
  lawful presence, citing 8 U.S.C. 1621, which bars people who are not lawfully present from
  state professional licences. BHEC applies the same rule. Neither agency certifies EMTs. [E] ✓
  Whether DSHS applies 1621 to EMS certification was not found either way.
- **DSHS forms:** search snippets say some DSHS EMS forms ask for an SSN or EMS ID and cite Texas
  Family Code ch. 232 for mandatory SSN disclosure. ~ (form URLs returned 404)
- **Schools:** TEEX requires "U.S. citizenship/permanent resident information" at registration.
  [E] ✓ Texas EMS School's online program is only for Texas residents. [E] ✓ No other school page
  read states a citizenship or SSN rule.

**What this means in practice** [O, my reading]: a U.S. citizen or permanent resident with an SSN
faces no barrier beyond the listed requirements. A visa holder or DACA recipient with an SSN
probably can certify, but this is not confirmed. Someone without an SSN or lawful status faces
real uncertainty at NREMT registration, the DSHS application and some schools. They should get a
written answer before paying tuition (see Open questions).

**Which is cheapest depends on where you live** [O]:
- If you live in a community college district, its in-district rate is usually the lowest price.
  Alvin's ~$871 was the lowest found (Tarrant's $444 in-county tuition is lower still, but it adds about $800 in listed extras). Prices for Houston CC, San Antonio College, El Paso CC and
  Austin CC were not published on the pages read, so check your own district's college.
- Otherwise, Dallas EMT at $995 is the cheapest published private course with stated DSHS
  approval. Its sister schools, Houston EMT and San Antonio EMT, state DSHS approval but did not
  publish tuition.
- RC Health Services, the example you named, was not the cheapest even at its 2018 price.

## Outdated or superseded material
- RC Health Services' price sheet is dated April 2018. Its fees (NREMT $70) are below today's
  NREMT fee of $104, so its tuition is likely out of date too.
- National EMS Academy's $400 promotional cost sheet expired January 1, 2025.
- Older SB 8 scholarship documents (2022–2024) describe terms that the current DSHS page no longer
  shows; the TWC program appears to be the current state funding route.

## Open questions
- **Can someone without an SSN or lawful status be certified?** Ask DSHS in writing:
  EMSCert@dshs.texas.gov or 512-834-6734. Ask NREMT whether it issues a pseudo-SSN to Texas
  candidates. Get both answers before paying tuition.
- **Will a criminal record be a problem?** Use the DSHS $50 prescreening before enrolling.
- **What does your local community college charge?** Look up its in-district EMT-Basic price; it
  is likely the cheapest option if you live in the district.
- **Is there a sponsored seat near you?** Ask local ambulance services whether they hold a TWC
  grant for the 2027 cohorts.
