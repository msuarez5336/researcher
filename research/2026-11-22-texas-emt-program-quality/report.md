## Top findings
1. **Most hands-on training: Dallas College.** Its EMT Basic Certificate is the longest program
   found (272 hours, with hospital rotations and an ambulance internship) and runs evenings,
   weekdays and hybrid. It costs an estimated $1,782 in tuition for Dallas County residents,
   plus about $365 in listed fees (about $2,150 in all). Its weak point is a below-average
   first-try pass rate in 2022 (66%; 83% within six tries). [E] ([Dallas College](https://dallascollege.edu/cd/credit/ems-paramedic/pages/emt-program.aspx), [DSHS 2022 table](https://kilgore.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2022NatRegistry.pdf)) ✓
2. **RC Health's rotations are real but not unusual.** RC states 48 ambulance hours (at least
   six transports) plus 24 emergency-room hours. Tarrant County College reportedly requires the
   same 72 hours, and Dallas College has far more total hours for a similar or somewhat higher
   price. RC's real
   advantages are flexibility (online, year-round, Irving campus) and a large track record. Its
   pass rate in 2022 was mid-pack: 73% first try across 708 students. [E] ([RC Health](https://rchealthservices.com/Live_Oak__TX.html)) ✓
3. **Best results on the exam: Tarrant County College and EMTS (Carrollton), 82% first try in
   2022.** UT Dallas's program (UEMR) was far ahead at 94%, but it is built for UTD
   undergraduates. [E] (DSHS 2022 table) ✓
4. **Cheapest private option, weakest results: Dallas EMT ($995).** Its operator, EMS University
   LLC, had a 49% first-try pass rate in 2022 (66% within six tries, 70 students). Its site does
   not state clinical hours or sites. [E] ✓
5. **Most programs don't publish clinical hours or name their hospitals.** Only RC (hours),
   Dallas College (total hours), UT Dallas (hours and six named hospitals) and Tarrant (hours,
   unverified) do. Ask any program you're considering for both before enrolling. [E/O]

For scale: the Texas rule as last codified (amended 2017) sets a 150-hour minimum course,
including supervised ER and ambulance experience, but no separate clinical-hour minimum. [E]
([25 TAC 157.32](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/25-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-157-32)) ✓
DSHS published a draft rule revision dated August 20, 2026, and a March 2026 presentation
reportedly proposed 192 hours with 48 clinical/field hours. ~ Whether that has been adopted
was not confirmed; if it has, programs below 48 clinical hours will have to add more.

## Pros and cons of the main candidates

**Dallas College: best for the most hands-on training**
- Pros: 272 hours, the most found; hospital and ambulance experience; evening and hybrid options;
  fees published (about $2,150 in all for a Dallas County resident).
- Cons: not cheap ($1,782 tuition); 66% first-try pass rate (2022), below the RC and Tarrant
  figures; it's a college
  semester schedule, not a rolling start; clinical hours by setting not published.

**Tarrant County College: best for exam results at a public price**
- Pros: 82% / 90% (2022); college-scheduled clinicals (reportedly 24 h ER + 48 h ambulance);
  published extra costs, so fewer surprises (listed as approximate, per semester).
- Cons: in Fort Worth; Dallas County residents pay out-of-county tuition (amount not captured);
  the listed extras add about $800.

**RC Health Services: best for flexibility, with a known clinical commitment**
- Pros: 72 clinical hours stated (with a minimum of six ambulance transports); online lectures
  at your own pace; Irving campus with year-round starts; largest program in Texas (708 tested in
  2022); strong ratings (4.8★ from 324, mostly Google reviews of the whole business).
- Cons: price unconfirmed (2018 sheet: ~$1,400–1,500, plus fees); 73% first try is mid-pack; no
  hospitals named; the hybrid-virtual track lists only 24 h of EMS time, so confirm which track
  includes the full 72 hours.

**EMTS (Carrollton): worth a call**
- Pros: 82% / 89% (2022) with 210 students; close to Dallas.
- Cons: price and clinical structure not published; its enrolment site refused access.

**Dallas EMT: cheapest private course, but a risk**
- Pros: $995; hybrid; three-day skills weekend.
- Cons: 49% first try in 2022; clinical hours and sites not stated.

**UT Dallas: if you are, or can become, a UTD student**
- Pros: 94% / 96%; the most transparent clinicals (named hospitals and a fire department rotation).
- Cons: aimed at UTD undergraduates; price and public access not stated.

## Recommendation for a Dallas student [O, my judgement]
1. **Most hands-on experience: Dallas College** (about $2,150 all in for a Dallas County
   resident, 272 hours). Ask for its current first-try pass rate and how clinical hours split
   between hospital and ambulance.
2. **Exam results are the tiebreaker: compare Tarrant County College and EMTS.** Get both
   quotes; Tarrant's out-of-county price and EMTS's price weren't published.
3. **Best balance of cost and experience if the price holds: RC Health Services.** It is
   flexible (online, rolling starts), and its 72 clinical hours are a genuine, quantified
   commitment, the same as Tarrant's. If its current price is still near the 2018 figure
   (~$1,400–1,500 plus ~$180 in fees), it costs less than Dallas College for the same clinical
   hours, though fewer total hours. Confirm the current price and that your track includes
   48 h EMS + 24 h ER.
   - If you live in Collin County, Collin College (~$600 + ~$200 fees, unverified) is the
     cheapest public option; ask about its clinical hours.
4. **Avoid choosing Dallas EMT on price alone**, given its 2022 results.

Questions to ask any program before paying: first-try NREMT pass rate for the last two years;
clinical hours split by hospital and ambulance; which hospitals and EMS agencies; who schedules
clinicals and the typical wait; total cost including fees; refund policy.
